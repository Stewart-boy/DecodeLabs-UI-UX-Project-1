📌 Project Overview

A new fitness startup wants to launch an app for busy professionals who struggle to find time for exercise despite long, sedentary workdays. The goal of this project was to step into the role of a UX Researcher and build a clear, evidence-based understanding of the target user before any design work began.

The project required developing a detailed user persona and a four-quadrant empathy map to explore the user's goals, frustrations, thoughts, feelings, and daily behaviours.

🎯 Target Audience

Busy, deskbound professionals who spend 8+ hours a day sitting with little to no physical activity, and who want to feel better physically without adding another demanding commitment to their schedule.

🧩 What's Inside

👤 User Persona
Alex Wisdom, a 31-year-old Financial Analyst who works 40+ hours a week at a desk, spends nearly all day on computer work, and has only about 10% energy left by evening — with effectively zero free time on weekdays and 8+ hours a day sitting, often 3+ hours without moving.

🧠 Empathy Map — A 4-quadrant map

The empathy map explores the user's overall experience through four key quadrants:

	•	💬 Says — "I don't have 45 minutes to go to a gym," "My neck and back are locked up after my morning meetings," "I'll try to start working out next week."
	•	👀 Does — Drinks 4 cups of coffee just to stay awake, eats lunch at his desk while checking work chats, sits for 3+ hours without standing, slumps on the couch scrolling by 6:30 PM.
	•	🧠 Thinks — "If I go offline for 15 minutes, my boss might think I'm slacking," "Is sitting like this ruining my back forever?"
	•	❤️ Feels — Stiff neck and tight shoulders, drained from jumping between calls and emails, guilty for not working out, annoyed by fitness apps demanding 30+ minute sessions.

🔑 Key Pain

"By the time I shut my laptop at 6:30 PM, I don't need a 45-minute workout. I just need 3 minutes to stretch my neck so I can breathe."

The central challenge isn't a lack of interest in fitness — it's that Alex's demanding, sedentary schedule leaves no realistic room for exercise in the form most fitness products assume.

💡 Key UX Insight

The user's challenge is not motivation but friction and format. A fitness experience aimed at this persona needs to fit inside the cracks of an already full day — seated, short, equipment-free — rather than compete with it as another obligation. This reframes the design direction away from "workout app" and toward "relief-in-place" micro-sessions.

🎨 Design Consideration

The findings from this research point toward solutions that are:

	•	Time-efficient (minutes, not sessions)
	•	Doable at a desk, with no special clothing or equipment
	•	Realistic and sustainable within an 8+ hour sedentary workday
	•	Simple to start without setup friction
	•	Supportive in tone rather than guilt-driven or streak-based

🛠️ Tool Used

Figma — persona layout, empathy map structure, visual design, and final export.

🧠 UX Process

	1.	Reviewed the project scenario and identified the target audience: busy, deskbound professionals.
	2.	Defined a specific user persona (Alex Wisdom) representing that audience.
	3.	Identified the persona's goals and daily frustrations.
	4.	Mapped the user's Says, Does, Thinks, and Feels across a typical workday.
	5.	Synthesized findings into a core quote, a key pain point, and a key UX insight.
	6.	Used these findings to establish a clear, evidence-backed understanding of the user before moving into the design stage.
